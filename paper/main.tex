\begin{abstract}
Vision-language-action models (VLAs) are widely reported to under-use their language input.
We ask \emph{when} this happens. Using minimal contrastive pairs on LIBERO-Goal---identical
observations, instructions differing in exactly one referent---we find that a LIBERO-finetuned
SmolVLA follows instructions well in the conditions such failures are usually attributed to:
$0.745$ on neutral states, and $0.93$--$0.95$ when caught mid-trajectory and commanded to
redirect, where the visual context actively opposes the instruction. Grounding fails only when
the instruction requires \emph{composition}. Commanding a pairing of familiar words never
demonstrated in training (``put the bowl on the rack'') degrades accuracy by $0.19$--$0.25$
under a paired control that holds the observation fixed and varies only the object word,
replicated across both publicly available competent checkpoints. Errors are structured:
$93$--$96\%$ send the arm to a destination that object \emph{was} trained with, indicating
substitution of a memorised pairing. A 130-site causal patching sweep did \textbf{not} localize
this failure, and we show its apparent structure is reproduced by a control in which no failure
exists. A linear probe confirms the instruction is encoded in the vision-language backbone, but
its expert-level results do not replicate across checkpoints, so we report no
readout-versus-encoding verdict. We release the stimulus generator, a checkpoint competence
gate, and the controls that caught five false positives in our own pipeline.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:intro}

VLAs map a camera image and a language instruction to robot actions. A growing behavioural
literature reports that they under-use the language and default to visual
priors~\citep{better,icbench,liberocf,robosem}. This matters because the instruction is the
entire control interface: a policy that ignores the words is not steerable.

That literature establishes \emph{that} failures occur, largely in aggregate. It says much less
about \emph{when}---under which linguistic demands grounding holds and under which it breaks.
The gap is consequential for mechanistic work, which needs a condition that reliably elicits the
failure before it can localize anything.

We probe with minimal contrastive pairs: identical observations, instructions differing in
exactly one referent, so any change in the predicted action is attributable to the swapped word.
Across three regimes of increasing linguistic demand, grounding holds in the two where prior work
would predict failure---including direct visual--motor conflict---and breaks only under
composition. The error pattern identifies the mechanism as substitution of a memorised pairing,
i.e.\ an object$\leftrightarrow$destination \emph{binding} failure rather than a failure to
attend to language.

\paragraph{Contributions.}
\begin{enumerate}\itemsep0pt
\item A controlled localization of \emph{when} VLA grounding fails: compositionally, not
  globally. Two null regimes are evidence, not discarded attempts.
\item A released, gate-validated contrastive stimulus generator, and a reference-free directional
  readout that makes undemonstrated commands scoreable.
\item A competence gate showing only 2 of 8 public LIBERO SmolVLA checkpoints are usable at all.
\item A negative localization result \emph{with the control that establishes it as negative},
  and a probe showing the instruction is encoded while the expert-level picture fails to
  replicate across checkpoints.
\end{enumerate}

\section{Related work}
\label{sec:related}

\paragraph{Behavioural failures in VLAs.} \citet{better,icbench,liberocf,robosem} report
instruction under-use. These characterise aggregate rates rather than isolating the linguistic
operation that fails, so they do not say which manipulations reliably elicit it.

\paragraph{Compositional generalisation.} Studied extensively in language-only and navigation
settings~\citep{scan,gscan}, and much less in continuous-control policies whose output is a
trajectory rather than a token sequence.

\paragraph{Binding.} Work on binding identifiers~\citep{bindingids,vlmbinding} predicts exactly
the object$\leftrightarrow$attribute failure we observe, but characterises it mechanistically on
the language-model side; the behavioural signature in an action policy has not been described.

\paragraph{VLA interpretability.} \citet{notallfeatures,vlatrace} localize features in VLAs.
Such methods need a condition that reliably elicits the failure, which is what we supply. Our
negative sweep also speaks to how readily these methods produce artifacts without matched
controls.

\section{Method}
\label{sec:method}

\paragraph{Minimal contrastive pairs.} One observation, two instructions differing in exactly one
referent. A pair ships only if it passes four gates, each with a regression test: a single-edit
word-level diff (one edit block, merged across filler words, preposition-free span, verb
unchanged); shared scene; both instructions achievable from the state; and counterbalanced source
tasks. Two of these gates exist because naive implementations silently admitted invalid pairs.

\paragraph{Three regimes.} \emph{Neutral}: pre-grasp states, where both instructions are
achievable and no visual prior favours either. \emph{Conflict}: post-grasp states, where the arm
already carries the object toward the demonstrated goal so the observation opposes the
alternative command. \emph{Compositional}: trained versus never-demonstrated pairings. All ten
LIBERO-Goal scenes contain the same seven objects, so novel pairings are physically executable.

\paragraph{Readouts.} For the first two regimes, \emph{directional IFR}: does commanding the
non-demonstrated instruction push the prediction away from the demonstrated trajectory (chance
$0.5$)? For the compositional regime, novel commands have no demonstration, so we score which
\emph{destination anchor} the predicted net displacement points toward. Anchors are the mean
final end-effector position of the demonstrations ending at each destination (sd
$0.03$--$0.05$\,m, pairwise separation $0.19$--$0.57$\,m; the cabinet anchor agrees within
$0.07$\,m across two different objects, so it tracks the destination rather than the task).

\paragraph{Controls.} A competence gate (Sec.~\ref{sec:exp}); pinned flow-matching noise, so
identical inputs give bit-identical actions (control passes at exactly $0.0$); a trained-pairing
control for the compositional readout; and a fixed-state control holding the observation
byte-identical while varying only the object word.

\section{Experiments}
\label{sec:exp}

\paragraph{Setup.} SmolVLA-450M on LIBERO-Goal, evaluated offline on stored demonstration
states. Paired bootstrap, 10k resamples.

\paragraph{Competence gate.} Only 2 of 8 public LIBERO SmolVLA checkpoints track the
demonstrations better than an unrelated trajectory does; four score \emph{worse} than that
baseline at every image orientation. All results below use the two that pass. We report this as
a result rather than housekeeping: it bears directly on the reproducibility of this literature.

\paragraph{The failure is compositional.} Table~\ref{tab:regimes}. Under direct visual--motor
conflict the policy redirects on command. Under an undemonstrated pairing of familiar words it
does not, and the gap is significant in all six destination\,$\times$\,checkpoint cells.
Crucially, $93$--$96\%$ of novel-command errors send the arm to a destination that object
\emph{was} trained with: the policy confidently executes a memorised pairing rather than
wandering.

\paragraph{Causal patching does not localize it.} Patching all 130 sites
($\{\textrm{vlm},\textrm{expert}\}\times L_{0..15}\times\{\textrm{resid\_pre},
\textrm{attn\_out},\textrm{mlp\_out},\textrm{resid\_post}\}$) from a working run into a failing
one yields an apparently clean picture: VLM influence decaying with depth, expert influence
rising toward the output. It is an artifact of causal proximity. Running the identical sweep on a
\emph{control} contrast---both instructions trained, no failure to recover---reproduces the same
profile (Fig.~\ref{fig:sweep}). Novel minus control has mean $-0.056$, and \textbf{0 of 130 sites
survive Benjamini--Hochberg correction} in either run.

\paragraph{Probing: encoded, but no replicable mechanism.} A linear probe trained on trained
pairings and tested on novel ones, with a label-shuffled control at every site and a
state-grouped split, decodes the named destination in the VLM at $1.00$ on both checkpoints
(controls at chance). The instruction is encoded and survives the backbone---though this is
close to definitional, since the destination word is present in the input tokens.

Inside the \emph{action expert} the picture does not replicate (Fig.~\ref{fig:probe}). One
checkpoint has 37 expert sites decoding novel pairings above $0.5$, peaking at $0.985$; the
other has 2, with 29 sites \emph{below} $0.10$ against a chance level of $0.25$---reliably
wrong rather than uninformative. \textbf{We therefore assert no readout-versus-encoding
verdict.} An earlier draft did, on one checkpoint alone.

Systematic sub-chance decoding is a lead rather than noise: with four balanced classes it
requires a consistent mapping to a wrong class. If the expert represents the destination the
policy will \emph{actually} move toward---the memorised substitute---rather than the one named,
a probe trained where the two coincide would predict the substitute and be reliably wrong
exactly where we observe it, matching the behavioural substitution result. Testing this means
decoding the executed destination rather than the named one, which we leave to future work.

\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}

Grounding in this VLA does not fail globally---it fails at composition. Under direct
visual--motor conflict the policy still redirects on command; under an undemonstrated pairing of
familiar words it substitutes a memorised destination. Causal patching did not localize this, and
a matched control shows the sweep's apparent structure is proximity rather than mechanism.
Probing instead finds the destination present in the action expert and unused.

\paragraph{Limitations.} Effect sizes are moderate and halved under the strictest control. Both
competent checkpoints come from a single uploader, so replication is weak. LIBERO-Goal supports
one object contrast, so results generalise over states rather than referents. Evaluation is
offline, so ``correct'' means heading toward the named destination rather than task success.
Finally, the probe decodes the named \emph{destination}, which is present in the input tokens; we
have not shown that the object$\leftrightarrow$destination \emph{binding} itself is represented.
