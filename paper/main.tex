\begin{abstract}
Robots controlled by vision-language-action models are given their tasks in ordinary English.
This paper studies when those words actually govern what the robot does. Recent work shows
that such models fail on object and place combinations they were never trained
on~\citep{brittle,scaleup}. Our contribution is to test several competing explanations for
that failure against matched controls, and to examine whether standard interpretability
methods can locate it inside the network.

Using a LIBERO-finetuned SmolVLA, we show the policy identical camera images paired with
instructions that differ in exactly one word. The failure is narrower than a
``vision overrides language'' account predicts. When the arm is already carrying an object
toward one goal and the command names a different one, the policy follows the command on
$94.6\%$ of trials $[91.7, 97.1]$, and on $74.5\%$ $[70.3, 78.8]$ of neutral
states~\citep{notallfeatures}. Failure appears only for untrained word combinations, and the
resulting errors are structured: they favour places the object was previously paired with, by
$11$ and $10$ percentage points above a uniform choice among the incorrect answers. The
behaviour is nevertheless not a fixed object-conditioned destination lookup, because which
place the policy heads toward still depends on which place the command names, selected on
$34$ to $67\%$ of untrained trials. Paraphrasing the instruction leaves the effect unchanged on
one of the two checkpoints; on the other it degrades even the trained pairings, which we report
as evidence that surface-form memorisation is a separable failure mode.

We then report two negative results together with the controls that establish them. A
130-site causal patching sweep appears to localize the failure, until the identical sweep on a
contrast containing no failure reproduces the same profile; no site survives correction. A
linear probe finds the instruction encoded in the vision-language backbone, but its results
inside the action expert reverse between checkpoints. We release the stimulus generator, a
competence gate that finds only $2$ of $8$ public checkpoints usable, and six measurement
artifacts that produced convincing but incorrect results in our own pipeline.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:intro}

VLAs map a camera image and a language instruction to robot actions. A growing behavioural
literature reports that they under-use the language and default to visual
priors~\citep{better,icbench,liberocf,robosem}. The instruction is the only channel through
which a user can change what the policy does, so a policy that ignores it cannot be redirected.
A kitchen robot that has learned to place bowls on the counter will keep doing so when asked to
place one in the sink, and no amount of rephrasing will help.

\citet{brittle} demonstrate the compositional case on SmolVLA, showing that held-out
object--location pairings fail because performance depends on correlations that do not
transfer, and \citet{scaleup} report the same across six models. Building on that result, we
address two questions. First, which competing accounts of the failure survive a matched
control? Second, what does it take to measure such a failure without arriving at a false
conclusion?

The second question is not incidental. Every intuitive measurement we constructed produced an
apparently clean and statistically significant number that later proved to be an artifact, and
in each case the error surfaced only through a check capable of contradicting it.

Our stimuli are minimal contrastive pairs (Fig.~\ref{fig:schematic}): identical observations
with instructions differing
in exactly one referent, so any change in the predicted action is attributable to the swapped
word. We test the competing accounts with matched behavioural contrasts. A
\emph{vision-dominance} account predicts failure when the visual context opposes the
instruction; we observe the opposite, with the policy redirecting mid-trajectory on $94.6\%$ of
trials. An \emph{attention-lapse} account predicts unstructured error; errors are instead biased
toward the object's trained destinations by $10$ percentage points above chance. A fixed
\emph{object-conditioned destination prior} predicts that the selected destination should not
depend on the command; it does, with the named destination selected on $34$ to $67\%$ of
untrained trials.

A failure rate alone cannot separate these accounts, because all three predict the same
aggregate degradation. What distinguishes them is the structure of the errors, which is why our
contributions below are stated as eliminations rather than as a single headline number. What
survives is command-dependent but substantially degraded behaviour on untrained pairings, which
we read as evidence of partial compositional competence. That reading is an interpretation of
behaviour rather than a measured mechanism: we do not claim to have identified an
object$\leftrightarrow$destination binding mechanism, and establishing one would require showing
that the relation is represented and causally drives the action, which our two mechanistic
analyses (\S\ref{sec:exp}) do not achieve.

\paragraph{Contributions.}
\begin{enumerate}\itemsep0pt
\item Evidence that the failure is not vision-dominance: under direct visual--motor conflict
  the commanded destination is selected on $94.6\%$ of trials, in a condition where language is
  reported to be ignored~\citep{notallfeatures}.
\item Evidence that it is not consistent with a fixed object-conditioned lookup either: on
  undemonstrated pairings the selected destination still depends on which one was named. Richer
  command-conditioned heuristics remain compatible with our data.
\item A checkpoint competence gate: only 2 of 8 public LIBERO SmolVLA checkpoints are usable,
  and four score worse than predicting an unrelated trajectory.
\item Two negative results reported together with the controls that establish them, and six
  measurement artifacts that produced convincing but incorrect results in our own pipeline.
\item A released, gate-validated contrastive stimulus generator, plus a reference-free
  directional readout that makes undemonstrated commands scoreable.
\end{enumerate}

\section{Related work}
\label{sec:related}

\paragraph{Compositional failure in VLAs.} \citet{brittle} report that held-out
object--location pairings fail on SmolVLA and $\pi_{0.5}$, and \citet{scaleup} find memorised
factor associations across six models; \citet{better,icbench,liberocf,robosem} document
instruction under-use more broadly; two of them go on to propose mitigations, attention
recalibration~\citep{icbench} and counterfactual-aware guidance~\citep{liberocf}.
\emph{What is missing:} the failure is characterised as an aggregate rate, which cannot
distinguish among the accounts that predict it, and the mitigations are proposed without one.
We contribute the controlled contrasts that separate those accounts, and we test two candidate
mechanistic analyses.

\paragraph{Interpretability of VLA grounding.} \citet{notallfeatures} run activation injection
on six models including SmolVLA and report that language sensitivity depends on task structure,
with a probe decoding a prompt at $99.3\%$ while behaviour ignores it. That probe result is
$\pi_{0.5}$-specific and correlational; our attempt to reproduce the analogous result on SmolVLA
inverted between checkpoints, which we report rather than resolve. Their layer interventions act
on visual and full-pathway activations, so where \emph{language} influence is lost remains open;
they name compositional instructions as future work.

\paragraph{Compositional generalisation.} Studied extensively in language-only and navigation
settings~\citep{scan,gscan}, and much less in continuous-control policies whose output is a
trajectory rather than a token sequence.

\paragraph{Binding.} Work on binding identifiers~\citep{bindingids,vlmbinding} predicts exactly
the object$\leftrightarrow$attribute failure we observe, but characterises it mechanistically on
the language-model side; the behavioural signature in an action policy has not been described.

\paragraph{VLA interpretability.} \citet{notallfeatures,vlatrace} localize features in VLAs.
Such methods need a condition that reliably elicits the failure, which is what we supply. Our
negative sweep also speaks to how readily these methods produce artifacts without matched
controls.

\section{Method}
\label{sec:method}

\paragraph{Minimal contrastive pairs.} One observation, two instructions differing in exactly one
referent. A pair ships only if it passes four gates, each with a regression test: a single-edit
word-level diff (one edit block, merged across filler words, preposition-free span, verb
unchanged); shared scene; both instructions achievable from the state; and counterbalanced source
tasks. Two of these gates exist because naive implementations silently admitted invalid pairs.

\paragraph{Three regimes.} \emph{Neutral}: pre-grasp states, where both instructions are
achievable and no visual prior favours either. \emph{Conflict}: post-grasp states, where the arm
already carries the object toward the demonstrated goal so the observation opposes the
alternative command. \emph{Compositional}: trained versus never-demonstrated pairings. All ten
LIBERO-Goal scenes contain the same seven objects, so novel pairings are physically executable.

\paragraph{Readouts.} For the first two regimes, \emph{directional IFR}: does commanding the
non-demonstrated instruction push the prediction away from the demonstrated trajectory (chance
$0.5$)? For the compositional regime, novel commands have no demonstration, so we score which
\emph{destination anchor} the predicted net displacement points toward. Anchors are the mean
final end-effector position of the demonstrations ending at each destination (sd
$0.03$--$0.05$\,m, pairwise separation $0.19$--$0.57$\,m; the cabinet anchor agrees within
$0.07$\,m across two different objects, so it tracks the destination rather than the task).

\paragraph{Controls.} A competence gate (Sec.~\ref{sec:exp}); pinned flow-matching noise, so
identical inputs give bit-identical actions (control passes at exactly $0.0$); a trained-pairing
control for the compositional readout; and a fixed-state control holding the observation
byte-identical while varying only the object word.

\section{Experiments}
\label{sec:exp}

\paragraph{Setup.} SmolVLA-450M~\citep{smolvla} on LIBERO-Goal~\citep{libero}, evaluated
offline on stored demonstration states. Paired bootstrap, 10k resamples. We use SmolVLA
because its small size makes exhaustive patching tractable on a single consumer GPU; the
discrete action vocabulary of larger autoregressive VLAs such as
OpenVLA~\citep{openvla} would permit a direct action lens, which we discuss as future work.

\paragraph{Competence gate.} A chance-level instruction-following rate is equally consistent
with a policy that ignores language and one whose predictions are noise, so before any grounding
claim we check that the policy tracks these states at all. The check needs no extra forward
passes. Writing $\hat{a}$ for the predicted 50-step chunk and $a$ for the demonstrated one at the
same state,
\[
\rho \;=\;
\frac{\operatorname{median}\lVert \hat{a}-a\rVert}{\operatorname{median}\lVert a'-a\rVert},
\]
where $a'$ is a chunk drawn from an \emph{unrelated} demonstration at the same stimulus states.
The denominator is therefore what ``a plausible trajectory for this scene, but not this one''
scores ($7.25$ over $n=50$ pairs here), which makes $\rho$ readable without a reference model:
$\rho \ll 1$ tracks the specific demonstration, $\rho \approx 1$ is no better than an unrelated
trajectory, and $\rho \geq 1$ is worse than one. We require $\rho < 0.70$ to run a checkpoint and
treat $\rho \geq 1.05$ as broken or misaligned. Only 2 of 8 public LIBERO SmolVLA checkpoints
pass, and all results below use those two\iflong~(Table~\ref{tab:screen})\fi. We report this as a result
rather than housekeeping: it bears directly on the reproducibility of this literature.

The gate was not tuned to the result it guards. It was added to diagnose the neutral and
conflict nulls, and the released commit history records the screen and the selection of the two
checkpoints 3 hours and 45 minutes before the compositional experiment was first written.

\paragraph{Statistical unit.} The data are nested: a LIBERO \emph{episode} yields several
stored \emph{states}; each state is evaluated under several \emph{instructions}; each
evaluation gives one \emph{prediction}. Predictions from one episode share a scene, an object
placement and near-adjacent timesteps, so they are not independent. Every interval reported
here is a cluster bootstrap over \textbf{episodes} ($50$ per source task, resampled with
replacement together with all of their predictions), $10{,}000$ resamples. Trial-level
resampling gives visibly narrower intervals; clustering widens all of them and moves one cell
(``the plate'' on checkpoint~A) from significant to not.

The Benjamini--Hochberg family for the patching sweep is the $130$ sites \emph{within a single
run}: novel and control runs are corrected separately, and the two checkpoints are corrected
separately, so no correction pools across conditions.

\paragraph{The failure is compositional, and not an artifact of one hard destination.}
Table~\ref{tab:regimes}. Under direct visual--motor conflict the policy redirects on command;
under an undemonstrated pairing it degrades sharply. The rack is the weakest destination even
when trained ($0.31$--$0.33$), so we report the gap with it excluded as well: the effect
survives at $+0.285$ and $+0.265$. The cleanest single cell holds the destination word fixed
and varies only the object---``the stove'', trained $0.890$ versus novel $0.560$.

\paragraph{Errors are biased toward previously paired destinations.} When a novel command
fails, the destination selected is more often one paired with that object during training than
a uniform random choice among incorrect destinations would give.

\emph{The null, explicitly.} For a trial naming destination $d$ with object $o$, the incorrect
options are the three destinations other than $d$; the chance rate for that trial is the
fraction of those three that appear with $o$ in training. We average this per-trial chance
across errors and subtract. For $\textrm{bowl} \to \textrm{rack}$ the bowl was trained with all
three other destinations, so that trial's chance is $1.00$ and it can contribute no evidence
whatever the policy does; for the bottle, two of three, giving $0.67$. Pooled chance is
$0.833$ against a raw rate of $0.944$. \textbf{The raw rate is therefore not the effect size};
the excess is $+0.111$ $[0.073, 0.144]$ and $+0.098$ $[0.056, 0.134]$.

\paragraph{The behaviour is command-dependent.} That bias is modest, and it is not consistent
with a \emph{fixed object-conditioned lookup}. Which destination the policy selects depends on
which destination was named: the bottle commanded to ``the plate'' is scored as heading there
on $0.45$ of trials and to ``the stove'' on $0.04$; commanded to ``the stove'' those become
$0.07$ and $0.56$ (checkpoint~A; the corresponding diagonal on B is $0.34$ and $0.67$). A
policy whose destination depended only on the object would give the same distribution under
both commands. This rules out that specific hypothesis; it does not rule out richer
command-conditioned heuristics that fall short of composing the relation, and we do not claim
otherwise. The exception is $\textrm{bowl} \to \textrm{rack}$, selected on $0.01$--$0.05$ of
trials---the one pairing for which we see almost no evidence of successful composition.

\paragraph{Paraphrase: robust on one checkpoint, uninformative on the other.} Swapping the
object word changes tokenisation and token count as well as meaning, so we repeated the test
with the command paraphrased: ``move the bowl to the rack'' in place of ``put the bowl on the
rack'', altering verb, preposition and length while naming the same object and destination.

On checkpoint~A the effect is unchanged: the gap is $+0.324$ $[0.272, 0.377]$ against $+0.358$
$[0.292, 0.425]$ under LIBERO's own phrasing, with dependence on the named destination if
anything stronger ($53\%$ and $69\%$ for the bottle). There, the degradation does not depend on
the exact string.

On checkpoint~B the comparison collapses, and instructively. Paraphrasing costs it not only the
novel pairings but the \emph{trained} ones, whose accuracy falls from $0.678$ to $0.406$ --
roughly the novel level -- leaving a gap of $-0.017$ $[-0.058, 0.025]$. The gap disappears
because the whole comparison degrades, not because novel pairings improve. Checkpoint~B was
trained from scratch on this task set, and this is what we would expect from a policy that has
learned the ten LIBERO strings rather than their content: a policy that cannot restate the
demonstrated tasks cannot be asked about undemonstrated ones. We therefore claim
phrasing-robustness for checkpoint~A only, and read checkpoint~B as evidence that surface-form
memorisation is a real and separable failure mode in this model family.

\paragraph{Two confounds this design does not remove.} An undemonstrated pairing is novel in
two ways at once: it lacks a demonstrated \emph{meaning} and a demonstrated \emph{trajectory}.
A policy that understood the command but could not generate a motor plan for it would produce
the same degradation, and we do not separate the two.

\paragraph{Geometry alone does not account for the result.} The readout scores whether
predicted motion heads toward a destination anchor, and in a fixed scene destination identity
is correlated with geometry, so a policy conditioning only on where things are might reproduce
the pattern. We tested this directly. A classifier given end-effector position, the distance
and unit direction to every anchor, and object identity, but never the instruction, predicts
the policy's own chosen destination at $0.546$ and $0.492$ on the two checkpoints, against
majority-class floors of $0.496$ and $0.412$: geometry buys $5$ and $8$ percentage points over
guessing the most frequent destination.

More decisively, such a model returns a single prediction per state, whereas the policy is
shown several instructions at each state and selects a different destination in $96\%$ and
$95\%$ of them. That variation is the effect this paper reports, and no state-only account can
produce any of it.

\paragraph{Causal patching does not localize it.} Patching all 130 sites
($\{\textrm{vlm},\textrm{expert}\}\times L_{0..15}\times\{\textrm{resid\_pre},
\textrm{attn\_out},\textrm{mlp\_out},\textrm{resid\_post}\}$) from a working run into a failing
one yields an apparently clean picture: VLM influence decaying with depth, expert influence
rising toward the output. It is an artifact of causal proximity. Running the identical sweep on a
\emph{control} contrast---both instructions trained, no failure to recover---reproduces the same
profile (Fig.~\ref{fig:sweep}). Novel minus control has mean $-0.056$, replicated at a second
trial count ($+0.046$), and \textbf{0 of 130 sites survive Benjamini--Hochberg correction} in
either run.

The observed profile follows from the architecture's intervention geometry. The action expert
reads the backbone through \emph{per-layer} KV entries, so a patch at VLM layer $i$ reaches
only cache entries $i{+}1 \ldots 15$: a patch at layer 2 propagates through thirteen downstream
layers, one at layer 14 through none. The decay therefore tracks how much of the network sits
downstream of the tap rather than how much instruction information survives to that depth. This
confound can arise in any encoder--decoder policy whose decoder cross-attends per layer, and a
profile reported without a matched control would there look graded and monotone while carrying
no localization content. We have tested one architecture, so we state the mechanism as a
hypothesis about intervention reachability rather than a general result.

\paragraph{Probing: encoded, but no replicable mechanism.} A linear probe trained on trained
pairings and tested on novel ones, with a label-shuffled control at every site and a
state-grouped split, decodes the named destination in the VLM at $1.00$ on both checkpoints
(controls at chance). The instruction is encoded and survives the backbone---though this is
close to definitional, since the destination word is present in the input tokens.

Inside the \emph{action expert} the picture does not replicate (Fig.~\ref{fig:probe}). One
checkpoint has 37 expert sites decoding novel pairings above $0.5$, peaking at $0.985$; the
other has 2, with 29 sites \emph{below} $0.10$ against a chance level of $0.25$---reliably
wrong rather than uninformative. \textbf{We therefore do not draw a readout-versus-encoding
conclusion.} An earlier draft did, on one checkpoint alone.

Systematic sub-chance decoding is unlikely to be noise: with four balanced classes it requires
a consistent mapping to a wrong class. \emph{One hypothesis}, which we state without endorsing,
is that the expert represents the destination the policy will actually move toward rather than
the one named; a probe trained where the two coincide would then predict the former and be
reliably wrong exactly where we observe it. We have not tested this---doing so means decoding
the executed destination rather than the named one---and it should not be read as a mechanistic
result.

\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}

Grounding in this VLA does not fail globally---it degrades at composition. Under direct
visual--motor conflict the policy still redirects on command. Under an undemonstrated pairing of
familiar words it succeeds far less often, with errors biased toward the object's trained
destinations---but it has not fallen back on a lookup, because which destination it chooses
still depends on which one it was told. What we can support is evidence of \emph{partial} compositional
competence, not a substitution policy and not an identified binding mechanism. Causal patching
did not localize the degradation, and a matched control shows the sweep's apparent structure is
proximity rather than mechanism; probing finds the destination decodable in the backbone but
gives expert-level results that invert across checkpoints.

\paragraph{Limitations.} Effect sizes are moderate. Both competent checkpoints come from a
single uploader, so replication is weak. LIBERO-Goal affords one object contrast
($\textrm{bowl}$ versus $\textrm{wine bottle}$), so results generalise over states rather than
referents; this is a property of a particular SmolVLA/LIBERO configuration, not a demonstrated
property of VLAs.

One confound stated in \S\ref{sec:exp} remains open: an untrained pairing is novel in meaning
and in trajectory at once, so semantic composition failure and motor generation failure are not
separated here. Resolving it needs a condition in which the two come apart. The geometric
confound is addressed in \S\ref{sec:exp} rather than left open, though our null is a linear
one, and a more expressive state-only model could close more of the gap.

Evaluation is offline throughout: ``correct'' means directionally consistent with the named
destination, not task success under closed-loop execution. Finally, the probe decodes the named
\emph{destination}, which is present in the input tokens; decodability is not causal
availability, and we have not shown that an object$\leftrightarrow$destination relation is
represented at all.

\section{Six measurement artifacts}
\label{sec:artifacts}

Every result in this paper had a predecessor that was wrong. We report them because five of the
six produced clean, statistically significant, entirely plausible numbers, and because the
property that caught them generalises: each was exposed by a check \emph{capable of
contradicting the headline}, never by re-reading code.

\paragraph{1. One-sided stimulus set.} The generator consumed task A's states before reaching
task B, and callers never took enough pairs to get there, so 100\% of states came from the
A-side task. The directional readout then only ever asked its question in one direction, and any
asymmetry between the two instructions read as a grounding effect. It produced a striking
destination-versus-object dissociation. \emph{Caught by} a second checkpoint returning the
opposite, equally significant result on identical stimuli.

\paragraph{2. Inverted images.} LIBERO stores frames under MuJoCo's OpenGL convention. The
policy saw upside-down scenes and scored no better than predicting an unrelated trajectory,
while still yielding confident instruction-following numbers. \emph{Caught by} a competence
gate---the ratio of prediction-to-demonstration distance against the distance between two
unrelated demonstrations---which we now run before any experiment.

\paragraph{3. A reference that rewarded stalling.} The directional readout compared a 50-step
predicted chunk against a \emph{single} demonstration action broadcast across all 50 steps,
scoring a policy that stops as highly as one that follows the trajectory through. \emph{Caught
by} asking what the metric would return for a degenerate policy.

\paragraph{4. A leaking probe split.} Each observation contributes eight examples (two objects
$\times$ four destinations); splitting by example put near-identical activations in both train
and test. Held-out accuracy read $1.000$ at \emph{every} site. \emph{Caught by} a site we could
verify carries no signal: \texttt{expert.L0.resid\_pre} is byte-identical across instructions,
so perfect decoding there is impossible. Grouping by observation dropped it to $0.400$, equal to
its own shuffled control.

\paragraph{5. A transplant that moved the wrong thing.} The injected delta was taken between
instructions differing in the \emph{object} word, so it encoded object identity alongside any
binding; injecting it degraded behaviour monotonically with dose while a norm-matched random
direction stayed near zero. Restricting to language-token positions localises this rather than
removing it, since the object token sits inside the language block. No dose or position slice
fixes it. \emph{Not yet fixed}: the design that would separate the two holds the words fixed and
contrasts trials where the same novel instruction succeeds against trials where it fails.

\paragraph{Determinism, and which backend to distrust.} Because flow-matching noise is pinned
and the instrumented forward is exercised against the stock implementation, the pipeline is
exactly reproducible: the full 130-site sweep run on CPU and on an A100 agrees to
\textbf{$0.0000$ maximum absolute difference across all 130 sites}. That matters for reading
the one backend that does \emph{not} agree---on Apple MPS we measured attention and MLP sites
diverging from CPU by up to $0.21$, while residual-stream sites agreed to $0.014$. Exact
CPU/CUDA agreement localises that to the MPS kernels rather than to the method, which is the
difference between a caveat that bounds a result and one that merely worries about it. Anyone
running interpretability sweeps on Apple silicon should check this before trusting per-site
numbers.

\paragraph{6. A headline statistic with an $0.83$ floor.} We reported that $93$--$96\%$ of
novel-command errors land on a destination the object was trained with, and read it as a
substitution signature. The number is almost entirely determined by the design. For
$\textrm{bowl} \to \textrm{rack}$, the bowl was trained on the other three destinations, so
\emph{every} possible wrong answer is a trained one and that cell contributes $1.00$ whatever
the policy does. Pooled chance is $0.833$; the excess is $+0.111$ $[0.073, 0.144]$.
\emph{Caught by} writing down the null---asking what the statistic would read if the policy
erred uniformly at random---which we had not done because the raw number looked decisive.
The lesson is narrow and cheap: a proportion whose denominator is constrained by the stimulus
design needs its chance level computed from that design, not assumed to be uniform.

\paragraph{What generalises.} A layer $\times$ component heatmap offers fewer opportunities to
visibly contradict itself than a scalar does. We would now require, before interpreting any such
map: a competence gate on the checkpoint, a matched control condition in which the effect being
localized does not exist, a replication checkpoint, and---for any direction-based
readout---conditioning on whether the instruction could move the action at all. That last one
matters because a site where language \emph{cannot} matter looks identical to a site where
language \emph{is ignored}.

\section{Extended results}
\label{sec:extended}

\paragraph{Per-destination compositional gaps.} The fixed-state control, which holds the
observation byte-identical and varies only the object word, gives positive gaps in all six
destination $\times$ checkpoint cells: the plate $+0.100 / +0.180$, the stove $+0.330 / +0.140$,
the rack $+0.320 / +0.260$. Pooled, $+0.250$ $[0.197, 0.300]$ and $+0.193$ $[0.143, 0.243]$.

\paragraph{Conflict strength.} Binning the conflict regime by how far the demonstration has
committed shows an apparent decline in the most-committed bin ($0.950 \to 0.900$ and
$0.975 \to 0.783$). This is a metric artifact rather than an effect: instruction sensitivity
collapses in the same bin, and where the instruction barely moves the action its \emph{direction}
is estimated from almost no signal. Splitting that bin by sensitivity separates them---at high
sensitivity, accuracy is $1.000$ and $0.949$.

\paragraph{Checkpoint screen.} Table~\ref{tab:screen} gives $\rho$ for every public LIBERO
SmolVLA checkpoint we could find. Two pass, one is marginal, four score worse than an unrelated
trajectory, and one fails to load. Two of the four failures are expected rather than surprising:
one is trained on LIBERO-Spatial and one on a single pick task, so neither is a claim about
LIBERO-Goal, and we list them only because a reader searching for a usable checkpoint will find
them. The remaining two are general LIBERO releases and their scores are not a preprocessing
artifact on our side---re-running both across all four image orientations leaves them near $1.8$
regardless, and \texttt{bicmol} is flat to three decimals across orientations, meaning the image
barely influences its output. The two checkpoints we use differ in training regime, one
finetuning \texttt{smolvla\_base} and one training from scratch, but share an uploader and
plausibly a data pipeline; agreement between them is a weak replication, not two independent
sources.
